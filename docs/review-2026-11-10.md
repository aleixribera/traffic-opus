# Kilometre Zero v0.0.99: review against Cities: Skylines and Transport Fever 2 (2026-11-10)

I played this as a fresh, demanding reviewer, using a real mouse and keyboard at 1600x900 in a headless, muted browser on an RTX 5060 (ANGLE/D3D11). I also checked 1440x900 and 1920x1080.

**What I played**
- A new company, "Tri-State Motor Freight", for about 50 real minutes, at speeds 1 to 4. I took it from an empty map to First steel.
- The generated late-game save (`tools/out/lategame.json`), for about 20 minutes: 3 towns, 3,495 buildings and 120 to 250 vehicles.
- No game code was changed.

**How the scores work.** Each category is scored against the game's own choices, as set out in `docs/direction.md`:
- It is a company game: there is no land to buy and no trucks to buy.
- Difficulty comes from traffic, not money.
- Towns grow in bands.
- Lights run on fixed timers.
- It is a PC game.
- The camera is a tilted diorama, with a straight-down Map view.
- All sound is synthesized.

CS means Cities: Skylines (1 and 2), and TF2 means Transport Fever 2. Screenshots are local only, in `.scratch/review/` (not committed). File names are given in brackets.

**Compared with the v0.0.79 play-test of 2026-11-09, this is a much better game.**

| | v0.0.79 | v0.0.99 |
|---|---|---|
| Village reached | 83 min | about 23 min |
| First steel | not reached in 160 min | about 49 min |
| Quickload | deleted every vehicle | keeps them (74 before, 74 after) |
| Catalog tab bar | moved between tabs | stays put |
| Steel-chain planks | 150, and the sawmill gate seized | 68, and the gate never seized |

The early and mid game now work. The weak spots have moved:
- **Late-game traffic.** Hard deadlocks never clear and spread.
- **Screen space while building.**
- **A long tail of stale or contradictory messages.**

**What I could not verify**
- **Sound.** The owner asked for a muted browser, so I judged sound from the code (`src/audio/`, about 4,100 lines) and the live audio graph, not by ear.
- **Frame rate.** The headless GPU run is capped at 144 fps by vsync, so I report frame-time spread and hitches, not headroom.
- **Not tested:** parallel roads, raised and sunken roads, buses, convoys, landmarks, the special industries and the roundabout tool.

### 1. Visual look and art direction: 7.0

**CS and TF2.** CS2 has a realistic town at street level and a dense, busy city from above. TF2 has a strong period look, vehicle models you want to follow, and terrain with real relief. Both fill the screen with detail at every zoom.

**This game.**
- The tilted diorama with Blender models reads well. Houses have chimney smoke, there are birds and cows, and the works sit in fenced yards with conveyors.
- At far zoom the land turns into an "atlas": hachured forest dots, outlined rivers and lakes, and town labels on Deco plaques [124-mapwide, 127-roaddone]. This is the best picture in the game and nothing in CS or TF2 looks like it.
- Autumn is lovely [155-autumn-day]. Night street lighting with lit windows is attractive [155-summer-night].

**Evidence against a 9**
- Town centres at 1,520 people are mostly empty green blocks with a few buildings on the edges [153-late-h300, 156-1080]. This is intended ("open grids, empty space"), but from the diorama it reads as unfinished rather than airy.
- The fenced works yards are half bare dirt pads until the site levels up [137-sawmill-dio, 138-sawmill-tilt].
- **Winter has a mask bug.** Snow covers open ground, but town lots and park blocks stay as large, flat, olive-brown rectangles. It looks like a texture failed to load [155-winter-day].
- The night Map view is very dark.
- Lighting jumps from dark at 03:27 to full daylight at 04:56, with no dawn.
- In one shot a bridge deck over water looked blue and see-through. This is low confidence; it may have been a construction ghost.

**To reach a 9**
1. Fill centre blocks with life rather than buildings: parked cars, market stalls, benches and paths, and lit squares at night. The "empty space" should look like it is used.
2. Fix the winter mask so lots and parks take snow.
3. Add a 30 to 45 game-minute dawn and dusk ramp.
4. Raise the night floor in Map view.
5. Give yard pads stacked goods and parked trucks that scale with stock, so a level-1 yard looks busy instead of empty.

### 2. Camera and controls: 6.5

**CS and TF2.** Both give smooth, fast WASD and edge-scroll panning that scales with zoom, a wheel zoom that never misses, middle-drag orbit, and a follow camera. CS2 also has photo mode.

**This game.**
- Rotate (Q/E), tilt (H/Y) and the Map view toggle (M) all work.
- The follow camera is good: "FOLLOWING Light truck · Food to Alder Run", then "Arrived · waiting for its next run".
- Fly-to buttons are everywhere: the NEAREST deposit pointer, "Fly there" on new towns, and the Traffic-stuck sign. They do a lot of the work that panning does in CS.
- The wheel has Auto, Zoom and Pan modes.

**Evidence against a 9**
- One tap of W or S held for 0.35 s panned about 3,400 world units. At most zooms that throws the view off the area you were looking at, so I gave up on keyboard panning and used fly-to buttons.
- Wheel notches sent in quick succession sometimes did nothing. With notches 0.35 s apart, zoom always worked. This may be a quirk of my automation, so I hold it lightly.
- **Labels catch clicks.** In Map view a town label swallowed the second click of a road (bug B6). It gave no feedback, and the road was simply not built.

**To reach a 9**
1. Make keyboard pan speed time-based and scaled to zoom (a fraction of the screen per second), with acceleration.
2. Add edge-scroll as an option.
3. Let map labels and plaques pass clicks through while a tool is in hand.
4. Show a cursor ring when the wheel zooms, to give feedback on missed notches.

### 3. Road building: 7.0

**CS and TF2.** CS has the deepest tool: straight, curve and freeform modes, upgrade-in-place, parallel snapping, elevation, and visible guidelines and angles. TF2 has the cleanest long-distance tool, with automatic bridges and tunnels and a slope readout.

**This game.** In the spirit of TF2 and helpful:
- It snaps to a works gate and prices the link: "To the gate $3,321".
- It builds automatic truss bridges with a readout: span, cost of the bridge alone, and steepest climb.
- Reject reasons are specific: "Angle too sharp (7°, min 24°)", "No room for the bridge's ramp", "Joins the dirt road at 60°".
- A 3.76 km dirt road to an iron mine took two clicks in Map view for $5,635. Demolition is priced before you commit: "Demolishes 2 buildings: $300 compensation" [125-longroad].
- A works dropped within 400 m of a road lays its own street.

**Evidence against a 9**
- The road catalog fills the bottom 40% of the screen (y > 515 at 900 px) the whole time you draw [125-longroad]. You end up drawing in the top half of the screen.
- Closing the catalog with R, or with a second Esc, drops the tool. To draw with a clear view you must close it, and then you have nothing in hand.
- At 4 km scale the end point is hard to place: a few pixels changed "Demolishes 2" to "Demolishes 5" [126-longroad2].
- The Map view label problem from category 2 makes it worse (bug B6).

**To reach a 9**
1. While a road or works is in hand, collapse the catalog to a one-line strip (the "Dirt road · $15/10 m · IN HAND" bar already exists) and expand it on hover or Tab.
2. Hide the catalog without dropping the tool.
3. Snap end points to the nearest road node within a pixel radius at far zoom. Show the node it will join, and say which houses will be demolished and why.

### 4. Traffic simulation and feel: 5.5

**CS and TF2.** CS2 has lane-level simulation, lane changes and turn lanes. Its jams build and clear, and in CS1 stuck cars despawn as a last resort. TF2 has simpler road traffic, but trucks rarely lock up for good. In both games a jam is a puzzle that clears once you fix it, and does not grow without limit on its own.

**This game.**
- Per-car motion is smooth.
- Night traffic is lighter. Vehicles fell from 250 to 120 overnight, a mild day/night rhythm.
- The **Traffic stuck** panel is the best traffic tool I have seen in this genre. It tells you:
  - how many vehicles are standing still;
  - which vehicle is first, and why it is waiting ("the road past the junction is full, so it waits for room. The jam starts further on: follow the queue to its head");
  - the longest wait;
  - which junctions are holding traffic.

  It also rings the vehicles and roads on the map [151-stuck].
- Lights run on fixed timers, as designed.
- In my own game the mid-game freight (34 trucks) moved without seizing. That is the v0.0.79 blocker fixed.

**Evidence against a 9: hard deadlocks that never clear and spread.** This is the lowest score in the review.
- In the late-game save, the cluster of three closely spaced junctions south-west of Poplar Creek locked up into a circular wait.
- Over 2 real minutes at speed 4 (about 11 game hours):
  - the longest standing car went from 245 s to 444 s;
  - "places stuck" grew from 3 to 6 to 8, and by the end of my session to 14;
  - the same cars stayed ringed throughout [151-stuck, then 152-stuck-after].
- The stuck cars sit on a mix of town-laid streets (segments 965, 949 and the avenue 1010, owned by town 3) and pre-laid streets (1008, 1009). So towns are laying junctions closer together than the traffic model can handle.
- Since difficulty is traffic, a jam you can fix is the game. A deadlock the player did not build, and that never clears, is a bug.

**To reach a 9**
1. Treat junctions closer than about 2 car-lengths plus the junction box as one node, with one controller and one "don't enter unless the exit is clear" check. The circular wait comes from each junction's check passing on its own.
2. The town road planner must not lay a junction within that distance of another.
3. Add a last-resort release: after N minutes stuck, the vehicle at the head of a cycle reroutes or turns around. Count it as a lost trip, so it still costs the player.
4. When the stuck panel sees a cycle, say "Deadlock" and offer the one-click fix: lights covering the whole cluster.

### 5. Freight and industry depth: 7.0

**CS and TF2.** TF2's chains are deep and its cargo flows are visible. CS industries are area-based with warehouses. Neither explains why a chain is stalled as well as this game does.

**This game.** Chains read clearly in the catalog through icons: Fertile soil → crops → food; logs → planks; iron ore and coal → steel and slag [133-processing]. Specific strengths:
- Plants within 60 m are linked by a conveyor automatically.
- Power works as a reach circle with a 40% fallback when out of reach. The site panel explains it: "Needs 6 MW: no power plant in reach, works at 40%" [122-ironghost].
- Each building site lists the planks it needs, and the goal panel's NOW line forecasts arrivals: "A load of planks is on the way from your Sawmill (2.3 km): about 6 hours".
- The mid-game chain (power plant, iron mine 4.1 km out, coal mine 3.6 km out, smelter) went from placement to First steel in about 15 real minutes at speed 4. Sites asked for 68 planks in total, not 150.
- Deposits sit kilometres apart, as the direction intends, and the NEAREST pointer flies you to them.

**Evidence against a 9**
- **A linked camp with no road does nothing (bug B2).** A logging camp placed more than 400 m from any road never got a street, even after a road reached the conveyor-linked sawmill. The sawmill sat on "waiting for logs… held up: no road to the gate". The conveyor drawn between the two buildings suggests they are connected, but logs do not flow.
- **Coal advice points at a locked building (bug B4).** The Coal Mine's NOW line says "no Cement Works to take its coal. Build a Cement Works", while a Power Plant and a Smelter that burn coal stand on the map.
- **Card text mismatch (bug B5).** The Power Plant card says "Burns coal for power"; the milestone banner and hand bar say "Coal or wood chips".
- **Contradictory messages.** The smelter said "waiting for coal" while the coal mine said "your plants that use coal have all they can store, with the loads on their way". The game is right, because loads are in transit, but it reads as a contradiction.
- A power plant beside the sawmill said "no road in reach". The fenced yard's internal roads don't count, and the ghost gives no preview of whether the plant will link.

**To reach a 9**
1. Let a conveyor carry goods even when one end has no road, or draw the conveyor dashed and greyed with "inactive: the camp has no road".
2. Point coal guidance at the nearest consumer that exists.
3. Unify the card text across the catalog, banner and hand bar.
4. Turn "all they can store" into "3 loads on the way to the smelter; the next lands in about 2 hours".
5. Show link and conveyor previews on the ghost while placing.

### 6. Town growth and life: 6.5

**CS and TF2.** CS towns grow lot by lot, with visible demand, zoning colours and pedestrians. TF2 towns grow by themselves and visibly react to the cargo you deliver.

**This game.**
- Towns grow in ribbons of houses along the player's connecting road.
- New towns appear on milestones (Danner Ridge, then Pecan Bend at 3.1 km), each with a banner and "Fly there" / "Open" buttons.
- The Towns window shows a sparkline and supply status.
- Alder Run went from 79 to 414 people and Danner Ridge from 0 to 299 within about 50 minutes, with no boom and bust.
- The late Poplar Creek (1,520) has a town hall square, a high street of brick shops and avenues [155-autumn-day].
- Growth is stated in words ("Danner Ridge needs more food. Your Food Plant is short of crops").

**Evidence against a 9**
- At street zoom a town of 1,500 is a grid of detached houses on large plots with empty blocks in the middle. Pedestrians, parked cars and street furniture are hard to find. CS at the same size feels inhabited.
- The town hall square in Poplar Creek is the only place that reads as a centre.

**To reach a 9**
1. Give the centre density bands a visible step: terraces and shops that join up along the high street as the band rises.
2. Put people on the pavements near shops and the hall.
3. Animate redevelopment (scaffold, then the new building) so a band change is a moment you see.

### 7. Progression, goals and long-term pull: 6.5

**CS and TF2.** CS uses population milestones that unlock services and map tiles, and the pull is building the city you imagine. TF2 uses eras: new vehicles every few decades change what is possible.

**This game.**
- Milestones list their unlocks "in the order to build them", with plank costs [135-sawmill, 140-steel2].
- The goal panel's NOW line is state-aware and gives ETAs.
- Pacing now hits the direction's targets:

  | Milestone | Real time |
  |---|---|
  | Growing hamlet, Sawn timber, new town | about 19 min |
  | Village | about 23 min |
  | Steady trade | about 37 min |
  | First steel and Two villages | about 49 min |

**Evidence against a 9**
- Several milestones can complete inside one game day at speed 4, and they stack ("Next (2 more)") while the game keeps running at speed 4 behind the modal (bug B7).
- After all 20 goals, the late save says "Every goal reached. Now the towns set the work", and the panel becomes a list of daily shortfalls ("Food 43 more loads a day…"). That is a to-do list, not a pull. Nothing says what the next big thing to aim for is.

**To reach a 9**
1. Queue milestone cards and pause while they show, or show them as a non-modal ribbon at speed 3 and 4.
2. After goal 20, keep one named long project always on show, such as a megaproject, a town order or the next town's size band, with a progress bar. The towns' demand list becomes the means, not the goal.

### 8. First hour and onboarding: 7.5

**CS and TF2.** CS1 had almost no tutorial; CS2's advisor and tutorials are adequate. TF2 has a campaign and contextual tips. Neither is great.

**This game.**
- First food was delivered about 7.5 real minutes in, and Village came at about 23 minutes (83 in v0.0.79).
- The "Fertile soil" pointer and NEAREST FOREST button fly you to the right ground.
- The placement ghost prices the street to the gate.
- Tips are state-aware and can be dismissed.
- Esc layering is right: the first Esc drops the tool, the second closes the catalog, and the pause menu does not open.

**Evidence against a 9**
- **The 400 m road trap.** Placing a logging camp more than 400 m from a road is easy, and recovering is not. The "No road reaches your logging camp yet" tip stays up, but nothing tells you that drawing a road to the sawmill won't fix it.
- The milestone modal fills the centre of the screen while the clock runs.

**To reach a 9**
1. When a works is placed more than 400 m from a road, show a dashed suggested route to the nearest road, with its price and an "Build this road" button.
2. Do the same from the tip.

### 9. UI and readability: 6.5

**CS and TF2.** CS2's info views and panels are dense but consistent. TF2 has clear, period-flavoured windows with good line charts. Both keep the play area mostly clear while you build.

**This game.**
- The Deco skin is consistent, with no generic AI look.
- The Traffic-stuck panel, the goal NOW lines and the site and power readouts are excellent.
- The layout holds at 1440x900, 1600x900 and 1920x1080 [156-1080, 157-1440].

**Evidence against a 9**
- The goals panel permanently takes about 20% of the width on the right. The catalogs take 40% of the height at the bottom while you build.
- Stale or inconsistent messages:
  - **B8:** the "Traffic is stuck in 5 places" toast against "14 places" on the sign.
  - **B9:** income shows −$154/h right after a quickload, when it had been +$1,698/h.
  - **B10:** "NEW TOWN 0.8 km" is the distance from the camera, while the tip says "1.9 km south-west of Alder Run".
- The "Another site is here" overlap warning is big and red for an everyday hover.

**To reach a 9**
1. Let the goals panel collapse to one line (title and NOW line) by default. Expand it on click.
2. Apply the catalog strip from category 3.
3. Measure every distance from the nearest town or road, never from the camera.
4. Rebuild the toast text from current state when the toast is shown.
5. Recompute the income rate from the saved history on load.

### 10. Sound: 7.0 (judged from code and the audio graph, not by ear)

**CS and TF2.** CS has a soft ambient city bed, building sounds and radio stations. TF2 has strong, distinctive vehicle sounds and period music. Both are sample-based.

**This game.** All synthesized, and broad:
- **Ambient beds:** wind, air, power hum, tyres, water, crickets and a town murmur.
- **Engines:** engine loops for car, light, heavy and train, plus 3 individually voiced passing vehicles.
- **Works beds:** 4 slots, each mapped by works kind: saw, furnace, crusher, turbine, pumpjack, kiln, glass, assembly, tractor.
- **One-off events (16 types):** bird, owl, horn and klaxon (more of them in jams), gate, clank, steam, blast, axe, rooster, cow, clink, build, shop bell, dog and children.
- **Seasons:** a season sting, and the air bed changes with season and time (dawn chorus, summer-night crickets).
- **Radio:** a seeded swing combo composer (blues, AABA, minor blues, ballad; walking bass; solos) played through an "old set" filter. Tune names such as "Tailgate Ramble", "Headlamp Blues", "Payday in Farrowton" and "Danner Ridge Pa…" use the player's own towns, which is a lovely touch.
- **Cost:** the live graph ran at 48 kHz and cost 0.02 ms per frame on average, with the worst frame at 51 ms (the radio) [`app.audio.cost`].

**Evidence against a 9**
- I could not listen, so mix balance, fatigue over hours and how well the synthesized engines read are all unverified. A 9 needs ears.
- There is no weather in the game at all, and so no rain or wind-storm sound. CS2 has weather.
- The worst-frame cost of 51 ms (radio) is a potential stutter source.

**To reach a 9**
1. A human listening pass at three zooms over a full in-game day.
2. Move radio rendering fully to the worklet or worker, so its 51 ms worst frame is off the main thread.
3. Consider light weather (rain beds, wet roads) as the next big atmosphere feature.

### 11. Performance: 7.0

**CS and TF2.** CS1 is fine on mid hardware until large cities. CS2 was notoriously heavy at launch. TF2 is CPU-bound late, but steady.

**This game.** The late save at speed 4 with 215 to 250 vehicles:

| Zoom (halfH) | fps | p50 frame | p95 frame | Draw calls | Triangles |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 250 to 300 | 141 to 144 (vsync cap) | 6.9 ms | 7.0 ms | 202 to 216 | 6.5 to 6.8 M |
| 1,200 to 1,800 | 144 | 6.9 ms | 7.0 ms | 372 to 433 | 6.9 to 7.5 M |

There were 196 textures and about 370 geometries. In the early game I measured 166 calls and 1.0 M triangles.

**Evidence against a 9**
- At speed 4 in the late save there are regular 63 to 70 ms hitches, 2 or 3 in every 15 s window, and once a 1.3 s stall right after changing zoom.
- 6.5 to 7.5 M triangles at every zoom is a lot for integrated GPUs, which the PC-only target still includes. I could only test an RTX 5060.

**To reach a 9**
1. Profile the periodic hitch (likely the hourly or daily sim tick: growth, redevelopment, routing) and spread it across frames or move it to a worker.
2. Warm up shaders and models at load so the first close zoom does not stall.
3. Add LODs or impostors for houses and trees beyond mid zoom, aiming at under 3 M triangles at far zoom.
4. Publish a low preset and test it on an iGPU.

### 12. Polish and bugs: 6.0

The v0.0.79 blockers are fixed: gate seizure, quickload deleting vehicles, moving tabs and the offset canvas. What is left is one serious bug (the deadlock, B1) and many small ones. Each of the small ones dents trust in the game's otherwise very good words (see section 3).

**To reach a 9:** fix B1, then sweep B2 to B10.

### 13. Identity: 7.5

**CS and TF2.** CS is the city builder; TF2 is the transport-era game.

**This game.** Kilometre Zero has its own corner:
- It is a freight company whose work grows towns, without buying land or vehicles.
- Difficulty is road throughput.
- The look is a Deco "Tri-State Motor Freight" world with an atlas map, a radio that writes swing tunes about your own towns, and warm diorama lighting.
- The Traffic-stuck panel and the NOW lines give it a voice: it explains itself in plain sentences, which neither CS nor TF2 does.

**To reach a 9:** lean into the moments that are only this game's.
- When a deadlock clears or a new town is founded, the radio could dedicate a tune to it.
- Convoys and special loads (untested here) should be events the camera and radio both mark.
- Make the atlas Map view a place you want to play in, not just a view to check.

## 3. Bugs found, with steps to reproduce

**B1. Hard traffic deadlock at closely spaced junctions; it spreads and never clears.** Severity: high.
1. Load the late save: `app.loadGame(await (await fetch('/tools/out/lategame.json')).json()); app.setMode('play')`.
2. Click the TRAFFIC STUCK sign (bottom right). It flies to the three-junction cluster south-west of Poplar Creek.
3. Run at speed 4 for 2 minutes.
4. Expected: the queue drains, or at least the standing time resets.
   Actual:
   - the same cars stay ringed;
   - the longest standing time climbs from 245 s to 444 s;
   - "places" grows from 3 to 8, and to 14 by the end of my session.

   The stuck cars sit on town-owned segments (965, 949, avenue 1010, all town 3) and on pre-laid segments (1008, 1009).

   Shots: 151-stuck, 152-stuck-after.

**B2. A conveyor-linked logging camp with no road never works, and nothing says so.** Severity: medium.
1. In a new game, place a sawmill near a road.
2. Place a logging camp within 60 m of the sawmill (a conveyor is drawn), but more than 400 m from any road.
3. Expected: logs move by conveyor, or the link is shown as inactive with a fix.
   Actual: the camp shows "No road to the gate", and the sawmill shows "waiting for logs… held up: no road to the gate" and later "no workers". The tip "No road reaches your logging camp yet" stays. A road to the sawmill does not help; only a road to the camp's own gate does.

**B3. Winter snow is missing on town lots and parks.** Severity: medium (visual).
1. Late save: zoom to halfH 110 over Poplar Creek.
2. Run `__env.set({hour:11, season:3, day:5})`.
3. Large, flat, olive-brown rectangles sit inside snowy ground.

   Shot: 155-winter-day.

**B4. Coal Mine guidance points at a locked Cement Works.** Severity: low.
1. At Village, place a Power Plant, a Coal Mine and a Smelter.
2. The Coal Mine's NOW line in the goals panel reads "no Cement Works to take its coal. Build a Cement Works…" (locked until Steady trade), even though the Power Plant and Smelter take coal.

**B5. Power Plant fuel text disagrees.** Severity: low. Industry → Logistics & Power: the card says "Burns coal for power". The Village milestone banner and the hand bar say "Coal or wood chips".

**B6. Map labels swallow tool clicks.** Severity: medium.
1. Press M for Map view and zoom out to about halfH 5,000.
2. Pick Dirt road (Straight) and click a start point in open land.
3. Click the end point on a town's name plaque.
4. Nothing is built and nothing explains why. Clicking 15 px below the plaque builds the road.

**B7. Milestone modals stack and the game keeps running at speed 4 behind them.** Severity: low.
