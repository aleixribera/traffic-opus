# Kilometre Zero: vision

Aleix owns this page. It changes through talks with the director chat and in no other way. It wins over every other doc; `docs/design.md` describes the systems as built.

## The game

A 1920s to 1940s freight company on a road map. You build roads and works. Your trucks haul freight through real traffic, and towns spring up and grow on what lands. Throughput is the difficulty, never money. The player has two verbs, *draw a road* and *place a works*. Everything else runs itself and says plainly what it is short of. PC only: mouse and keyboard, 1440×900 and 1920×1080.

## Rules (settled; do not reopen)

- A company game, not a city builder. Towns grow themselves and spring up on milestones. No land to buy, no trucks to buy, no micromanaging single buildings. Freight finds its own route.
- Hard because of traffic. A jam costs throughput (slower deliveries, so slower growth and income) and is never a dead end: no permanent gridlock, no bankruptcy from traffic. The game always names the cause and offers a fix.
- Never a waiting game: at speed 4, a player is never more than about 3 real minutes without something worth building or fixing, building sites included.
- Every new game, the first one included, gets its own random map.
- Fixed-time lights, 24 s a phase. Smooth driving for every car is the bar for traffic.
- Towns grow steadily on proven supply, with no credit for goods still on the road. Old staples stay light as towns grow; growth comes from new resources.
- A mild daily rhythm; realism never changes which route a vehicle takes.
- Town orders are rare. Seasons last 16 days, and autumn is a freight rush.
- The look: a tilted Diorama camera, with a straight-down Map view one key away. A world that moves (smoke, trees, birds, dust), the 1930s on the street, real Blender models, and textures made by Claude only.
- All sound is made in the browser, with a wireless set playing period music.
- The map speaks: little help text, one thing at a time from the tools, and a need shown where it is.

## Stages

1. **The first two hours, plus tools for big networks** (now). Most of the weight goes on the first two hours, from the first road to the first tier-3 product: no waiting, a first jam by about 40 minutes that the player can manage, and a fresh map each game. Alongside that, the tools that keep later traffic manageable: ready-made interchanges (diamond, trumpet, cloverleaf), and bigger truck classes as sites level up. Whatever else is weakest gets fixed along the way.
2. **The mid game** (tiers 3 to 5, days 10 to 40), played and fun: long goals worth chasing, never padding, with convoys and landmarks proven in a real game.
3. **The late game.** Freight rail with level crossings that jam until a bridge replaces them.

Maybe later: river barges and lift bridges, choosing a start region, first chains that vary with the start, truck-only roads.

## How we know it is getting better

There is no fixed score to reach; each stage leaves the game better than it found it.
- At the end of each stage, a fresh reviewer plays a real new game (no generated save) and scores the 13 categories against Cities: Skylines and Transport Fever 2. No category may drop, and the stage's focus must rise.
- The reviewer notes the minute it got bored. That minute must come later with each stage.
- Sound is fine as it is (Aleix, 2026-11-13). It is not scored until someone listens.
